How this works

The estimate comes from a logistic regression trained on the Survey of Consumer Finances 2007–2009 panel, in which the Federal Reserve interviewed the same 3,857 families in 2007 and again in 2009. The model learns how answers given in 2007 relate to missed loan payments reported two years later, with dollar amounts converted to 2025 dollars. Its overall level is anchored to 2025, when about one in five U.S. households reported a late payment.

  • What matters most: a recent missed payment, little cash savings, more debt (especially student loans), and education. Income adds no information once savings and debt are known.
  • Missing answers are set to the U.S. average for that question, so the estimate becomes less personal the more you skip.
  • Privacy: the calculation runs entirely in your browser; your answers are not stored or sent anywhere.
  • This is a statistical estimate for households with your profile, not a credit score or financial advice.
